HOW THIS TOWN WORKS ✕
  • The goal: Bank the most policy victories by Election Night, November 2028 (22 months). A victory is credited to the single top contributor on a fight's winning side — the leaderboard in HQ REPORT tracks the race.
  • The loop: Scholars produce ✦ influence each month. Spend influence on policy fights and on courting donors. Donors pay the bills so you can hire more scholars. Victories cost influence the machine never gets back — that tension is the game.
  • Support: Each ops staffer supports 2 scholars. Unsupported scholars (bottom of the roster) produce at half rate — and quit after 2 straight unsupported months.
  • Raids: Candidates marked AT [RIVAL] currently work for the competition. Hiring one costs a 1.5× signing bonus and permanently dents that rival's monthly influence budget.
  • Fights: Commit influence to a side. When the clock hits zero, the bigger pile wins. If your side wins, you get the reward scaled by your share of the winning side's total — cash, clout (✦), and sometimes a free scholar, a warm donor intro, or donor amnesty. Rivals commit every month — the ⚑ line under each side shows exactly who's backing it.
  • Expertise: Every scholar whose tag matches a fight amplifies influence you commit there by +10% (max +50%) — shown as the ★ chip on the card.
  • Partisan fit: Scholars who share your institution's politics sign for 50% less; aisle-crossers cost +50%. Simpatico donors court 30% cheaper; opposed ones +40%. The ▼/▲ tips on market cards show the exact before → after price.
  • Donors: Courting costs influence; grants arrive monthly. Every donor has a demand — a scholar on the roster, a vanity program, monthly influence pushed into their pet issue, or ideological purity (backing a side they hate = instant strike). Unmet demand = 1 strike per month. Two strikes and they walk. Grants also run on cycles (12–24 months) — when one ends the donor departs amicably, so keep courting.
  • Payroll creep: Every December, everyone gets a raise (+8%). Standing still is a slow way to lose.
  • Programs: Money sinks (gala, podcast, lobby). Mostly useless for policy — but certain donors won't fund you without them. That's the game.
  • Losing: End two consecutive months with a negative treasury and the institution folds before the election. Otherwise, Election Night ranks everyone by victories banked.
