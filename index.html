HOW THIS TOWN WORKS ✕

New here? The Tour button walks the panels one by one. This is the reference.

1 · THE GOAL

January 2027 to Election Night, November 2028 — twenty-two months. Bank the most policy victories and be named the most influential think tank in Washington. A victory is credited to the single top contributor on a fight's winning side (ties go to you); merely backing the winner still pays cash and clout by your share, but not credit. The HQ leaderboard tracks all seven institutions.

The final six months are Election Season: rival spending runs 50% hotter. Rival budgets also drift upward all game, and whenever you lead the board (🔥) the town gangs up — rivals spend harder and counter-bid the sides you're winning.

2 · THE ENGINE

Scholars mint ✦ influence. Influence wins fights and courts donors. Donors pay grants that fund staff. Every point spent on glory is a point not invested in the machine — that tension is the whole game.

Ops staff support scholars (capacity on the card). Unsupported scholars — newest hires lose coverage first — produce at half rate and quit after two straight months. Morale: lose a fight you contested and that issue's scholars drop 25% for two months; lose the issue again while they're down and some leave. A win in their field restores them. Contest a fight with no bench at all (⚠) and a loss demoralizes the entire roster.

3 · FIGHTS

Four fights are live at a time. Commit ✦ with +5 / +25. The bar and percentages are live win odds: influence shares on a sharpened curve (a 2:1 lead wins about 90%), and when the ⏳ clock hits zero the wire rolls — upsets happen, and the Bugle animates every roll. The ⚑ lines show exactly who backs each side.

Rivals sit out fights beyond their pet issues, so their piles run deep where they do show up — and they surge into a fight's final month, so a last-minute dump meets a wall. Your bench amplifies commits: +12% per matching scholar (cap +60%), plus the War Room, a Gov Relations Lead, or a MEGAPHONE donor. Rewards scale by your share; some fights pay in kind — a free scholar, a warm donor intro, or amnesty on donor strikes.

4 · STAFF

Scholars: the tag is their expertise; the lean sets partisan pricing (▼/▲ tips show the exact discount or premium, and hardline ◀◀/▶▶ shops feel both harder). 🔥 DIVAs produce enormously and drive colleagues out. Candidates marked AT [RIVAL] are raids: a 1.5× signing bonus, and that rival's budget takes a permanent dent. Some candidates are simply bad deals — no chip, just numbers that don't add up.

Ops: support generalists (1–3 scholars) or zero-support specialists — comms, development, editor, creative, gov relations — plus a consultant who does nothing. CHAOTIC ops sometimes deliver no support at all; EXPENSIVE ones bleed overhead. Rivals periodically bid for your scholars: match (a permanent raise) or let them walk — an unanswered bid defects. Letting ops go costs only severance; letting a scholar go unsettles donors.

5 · DONORS

Court donors with ✦ (partisan pricing applies). Grants pay monthly on a 10–20 month cycle; when it ends you get a month to renew at half cost on stricter terms (one strike ends a renewed deal) or let it lapse. Every donor has a demand — a scholar on staff, a program running, monthly influence in their pet issue, or ideological purity. An unmet demand is a strike; two and they walk. Perks and flaws are called out (ANCHOR, MEGAPHONE, MATCHING; MEDDLER, FICKLE, JEALOUS), and some blue-chip patrons appear only once your base qualifies.

Donor Confidence (HQ Report) is one gauge for the whole base. Departures, firings, courting more than one donor a month, and holding more donors than your development staff can steward (base 3, +2 per Development Director) all shake it; renewals and banked victories restore it, and worries fade slowly. A Watchful base throws nervous strikes; a Spooked one charges more to court and double to renew; in Exodus a donor leaves every month. Parting with a donor also demoralizes the scholars in their issue.

6 · PROGRAMS & PROSPECTING

Programs (the PROGRAMS button) are money sinks with a purpose: vanity programs exist because certain donors demand them; long-term ones — the journal, the War Room, junior fellows, the endowed chair, the West Annex — trade heavy cost for compounding advantage. A Creative Director makes them cheaper. Stale market? PROSPECT re-deals hiring or donors for cash and influence, and each sweep costs 50% more than the last.

7 · THE CALENDAR, CRISES & THE BUGLE

Washington has rhythms. The State of the Union (February) lands a marquee fight with double rewards; the August recess brings no new fights and 20% cheaper courting; November 2027 prints the one-year-out standings; and in March 2028 a presidential campaign wants your endorsement. Rival moves and lead changes make the paper too.

Now and then the Bugle prints an EXTRA — a scandal, a funder feud, a shutdown that freezes the Hill, a surprise recess — and you must pick your poison before the next month begins. Paid exits scale with your size (a share of treasury or production); free exits hurt differently. The ticker logs everything; the month-end front page reports resolutions, departures, raises, and the verdict on Election Night.

8 · MONEY & LOSING

The HQ ledger shows grants, payroll, rent (a flat monthly figure per institution), and programs. Every December payroll rises 9%. End two consecutive months in the red and the institution folds before the election. Otherwise, November 2028 ranks everyone by victories banked — and the top of that list is the only thing that counts.
